An AI policy that its own leaders do not follow is no longer a policy.

25 September 2026

Short version →

A WalkMe study from July 2025 finds that 78% of employees use AI tools their company has never approved, and that 93% of the executives surveyed do the same. The issue is not team discipline but the governance around it. Three reflexes: acknowledge real usage before banning it, offer safe and simple tools, and start by examining the leadership team’s own practices.

An AI policy is drafted in the executive committee. In the lift afterwards, everyone goes back to their personal AI tool.

A WalkMe study run in July 2025 confirms it at scale: 78% of employees use AI tools their company has never approved. Even more surprising, 93% of the executives surveyed do exactly the same.

🔍 Why is shadow AI not a question of discipline?

Because usage is massive, and shared all the way to the top of the organisation. When nearly everyone surveyed works around the rule, this is no longer a problem of individuals: it is a sign that the rule does not meet the need.

The issue is not team discipline. It is the governance around it.

An AI policy that its own leaders do not follow is no longer a policy. It is a shared fiction.

— 01 —

🎯 Which reflexes change the game?

Why acknowledge real usage before banning it?

Because it already exists, whether we like it or not. Banning a use you do not know about pushes it into the shadows. Mapping it, without hunting for culprits, finally gives an accurate picture of what teams actually do.

How do you offer safe tools that are simpler than the ones people hide?

By providing approved tools that are as quick to pick up as personal ones. If the safe option is heavier than the hidden one, the hidden one wins. The regulatory framework (AI Act, GDPR) is set out in our AI Act and GDPR guide for an SME.

Why start with the leadership team’s own practices?

Because consistency starts at the top. An executive committee that looks honestly at its own usage gives the policy a credibility that no internal memo can replace.

An organisation never secures what it refuses to look at. With AI, prevention is decidedly better than cure.
