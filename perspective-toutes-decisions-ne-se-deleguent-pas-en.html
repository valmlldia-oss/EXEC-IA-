Not every decision can be delegated. Not even to an AI.

Published on LinkedIn · September 8, 2026

I was fortunate to spend much of my career close to senior executives. I learned a great deal alongside them, especially about what makes a decision complex when the data do not all point the same way.

A strategic direction.

A major investment.

A reorganisation.

A decision that affects people.

A recent Morgan Stanley study on AI governance offers a useful insight: 41% of the executives surveyed see human oversight of high-risk situations as the most important governance rule.

That figure, to me, points to the real question: how far should you delegate?

AI can analyse, compare, simulate, challenge an intuition and bring options to the surface.

⚖️ But the more a decision commits the company,
the more the final call must stay human.

— 01 —

Because an executive does not decide with data alone. They decide within a context, with responsibility, and they answer for the consequences.

🎯 That is where I would draw the line:

AI informs the decision. The executive keeps the final call.

As AI advances, knowing what to delegate to it, and what never to delegate, will become, in my view, an executive skill.

💬 And you, which decision

would you never delegate to an AI?

Source: Morgan Stanley, “AI Governance Survey”, 2026.
