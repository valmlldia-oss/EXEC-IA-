Can your AI project be funded?
Yes, but not necessarily
the one you have in mind.

Expanded version of a LinkedIn post · September 25, 2026

Short version →

Yes, an AI project can be funded, but not necessarily yours, not for every company and not every service. The Diag Data IA scheme funds a technical analysis carried out by an approved expert. The Osez l’IA plan lists ready-to-use solutions and approved-expert support, not open consulting. A subsidy should never determine your project: first choose the problem to solve, then look at what support applies.

It all started with an audit of my site’s visibility on Google and in AI answers. One question surfaced: what questions do my future clients really ask?

Among them: “Is there any funding for my AI project?” Good question. So I went and looked more closely.

Osez l’IA, France 2030, Bpifrance, Diag Data IA, France Num, regional schemes… The options exist. But behind the word “funding”, the realities differ widely: eligible company or not, fundable service or not, provider freely chosen or selected, funding rate, out-of-pocket cost.

💶 Should funding determine your AI project?

No, never. The order should stay the same:

  • what problem do you want to solve?
  • Where can AI really create value?
  • Which project should be prioritised?
  • And only then: which schemes may apply?

A project built to fit a scheme risks solving no real problem.

What does Diag Data IA fund?

For SMEs and mid-sized companies with 10 to 2,000 employees, Bpifrance’s IA Booster programme, under France 2030, offers free awareness-raising, then a Diag Data IA co-funded at 40%: €10,000 excl. VAT, of which €6,000 excl. VAT is paid by the company. The scheme funds a technical diagnosis carried out by a Bpifrance-approved expert. Access is subject to eligibility conditions, notably minimum turnover of €1 million and independent-company status.

The provider is therefore not freely chosen, and the diagnosis does not replace the strategic choice upstream: which use to prioritise, and why that one.

Which main families of support exist for an AI project?

Three families stand out, each with its own logic.
Diagnostic schemes fund an analysis, such as Diag Data IA.
Solution catalogues list ready-to-use products, such as one of the two strands of the Osez l’IA plan.
More general national or regional schemes support digital transformation or investment.

None of these families is interchangeable with the others. Confusing a funded diagnosis with a funded deployment is one of the most frequent mistakes.

Why is the provider question decisive?

Because, depending on the scheme, the provider is either freely chosen or selected from a list.
In the first case, you can pick the one who understands your business.
In the second, the choice is framed, and you need to check that the proposed provider meets your need.

Funding obtained for a provider who does not fit the project costs time, even when it costs no money.

How do you combine a funded diagnosis with a strategic project? By keeping each in its place. The technical diagnosis clarifies what is possible with your data and tools. The strategic choice decides what deserves to be done, in what order, with what means.

The second ideally comes before the first: a diagnosis well targeted on an already prioritised use brings far more than a general diagnosis with no precise question.

Should you wait for funding before starting?

Not necessarily. Waiting for support can delay by several months a project whose value is already clear, and meanwhile the problem stays whole. The right question is not “support or no support” but “what first step can I take now, whatever the support?”.

A modest first use, run on a precise task, brings lessons that no application file can replace. It also makes the next step easier to defend, including in front of a funding body.

Does “Osez l’IA” fund any kind of consulting? No. The national Osez l’IA plan has two strands. The first is a catalogue of ready-to-use AI solutions for SMEs and mid-sized companies (assistants, automation, document analysis): these are products. The second groups support delivered through Bpifrance, such as Diag Data IA and the AI Accelerator, carried out by approved experts.

In both cases, the provider is not freely chosen. A strategic scoping workshop such as Cap IA is neither a catalogue product nor an approved expert’s service: it falls outside these schemes and should not be presented as fundable by them. EXEC’IA’s AI Agents, by contrast, are product solutions: they could fall under a catalogue of this kind, provided they are selected.

Which questions should you ask before filing an application?

Five checks avoid unpleasant surprises:

  • is your company eligible (size, turnover, sector)?
  • Is the intended service fundable?
  • Can you freely choose your provider, or is it imposed?
  • What is the funding rate?
  • What is the out-of-pocket cost, and for what result?

Precise conditions are published by the bodies themselves (France Num, Bpifrance, entreprises.gouv.fr) and change over time: check them at the source, when you apply.

Where do you start, in practice? With the problem, not the counter. State what AI should change in your business, identify where it creates value, prioritise a first project. Funding comes afterwards, based on that project.

Funding and compliance should both be anticipated at the scoping stage: for the second, see the AI Act and GDPR guide for an SME.

Which mistakes should you avoid when looking for support?

Five mistakes come back: building a project to fit a scheme, ignoring the out-of-pocket cost, confusing diagnosis and deployment, forgetting compliance (AI Act, GDPR) at the scoping stage, and starting too late.

The last deserves a systematic check: depending on the scheme, the application sometimes has to come before the service starts. That rule is found in the official conditions, before any commitment.

What if your company is not eligible? Do not give up on the project. An ineligible company can fund a first use with its own means, starting small: one task, one team, one measurable result. That is, incidentally, the best way to find out whether the project deserves a wider investment.

The absence of support is not a verdict on the project’s value. It simply changes the pace and the size of the first step.

How do you keep your bearings among so many schemes?

By coming back to the starting question: what problem do you want to solve? Schemes are many, their conditions change and their vocabulary shifts. The problem to solve stays the same, and it is what should guide the choice.

A leader who has clarified their problem reads a catalogue of support as a list of options. One who has not reads it as a list of constraints.

Which three things should you keep in mind?
The project must exist without the support, even if smaller.
The conditions must be checked at the source, on the date of filing.
Compliance must be thought through at the scoping stage, not after deployment.

If these three points are in place, the support becomes what it should be: an accelerator for a project already chosen, not the reason for choosing it.

What place for strategic support before funding?

A place upstream, on the question that schemes fund least: where does AI create value in your organisation, and in what order should you act? That is the role of a scoping workshop such as EXEC’IA’s Cap IA.

Once the problem and the priority are clear, the funded diagnosis, the chosen solution and any support fit together much more simply. You choose with a direction, instead of enduring a menu.

How do you present your project to a funding body? Problem before solution: what you want to change, for whom, and how you will know it worked. A funding body backs an expected result more than an intention. A file that starts from a clear need, a limited scope and a simple success criterion reads faster and is easier to defend.

Also prepare answers to your own questions: who will carry the project internally, how much time it will take, and what you will do if the result disappoints. These elements reassure as much as a figure.

What do you do once the funding is granted?

Funding pays for a step, not for what follows. Plan from the outset for what happens next: who uses the result, who develops it further, and how you decide whether to continue. A diagnosis left in a drawer has no effect on the business.

Go back to the original order: problem, value, priority. It will tell you whether the funded step was the right one and what the next one is.
